How to read this

The pick is the side and the line it was made at. If your book has a worse number, the edge is smaller.

Model is the model's own projected margin or total; edge is how far that is from the line, in points. Totals need 3+ points of edge (5+ in the season's first two weeks); spreads need 4+ and only run in November–December.

2× ref crew: the officials posted before tip and historically lean the same way, so the model would bet it again.

Late: the line first showed up on game day, after the best value is usually gone.

Range bar: where the final total (or the picked team's margin) is likely to land. The dark band is the middle half of past results like this one; the light band extends it to the middle 8 in 10, so 1 in 10 lands past each end. The center leans only part of the way from the line toward the model, because that's how much of the model's disagreement held up in testing. "% to hit" is the chance the pick wins from that range.

Similar past games: three earlier games with a close line where the model disagreed by about as much, with what the model said then and how it finished. Three games are color, not evidence.

Units assume 1 unit risked per pick at −110.

For fun. A model, not a guarantee. Bet only what you can afford to lose.
