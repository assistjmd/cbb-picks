Read this table carefully. The top rows are mostly against the opening line, the first number a sportsbook posts. Those lines move fast and often carry low limits, so most bettors can't get them. Picks here are posted the evening before the game, so the evening line rows are the honest measure of what you could have bet. The closing rows show the edge mostly disappears by tip-off, except on the biggest early-season spread edges. Picking the best five out of many variants also flatters them a little; the per-season results show which ones held up every year.

Why are there no moneyline picks?

The model's win probabilities were tested against the market's and weren't better, so moneylines lose to the bookmaker's cut. The model's projected margin is still shown for every game.

How much should I read into one row?

Not much, unless the sample is big. With 100 picks, a true 53% picker lands anywhere from about 43% to 63% just by luck. The "±" column shows that luck range (95%) for each row. This season's rows are especially small, so treat them as a check that nothing is broken, not as proof.
